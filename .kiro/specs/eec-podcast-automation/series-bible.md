# YALLA FLUENT — SERIES BIBLE
### *The 90-Day English Drama*
**Empire English Community (EEC) | Bilingual English-Learning Drama Podcast**

*Mission: “Learn with fun.” A story so good people binge it, that teaches real English by living it. Real value, never AI-slop.*

> **Show name:** **Yalla Fluent** (يلا Fluent) — *yalla* = "let's go / come on": instant
> Arab warmth + forward momentum. **Brand note (important):** keep it PREMIUM — gold-on-black,
> a respected academy, never cheesy — so the everyday word reads aspirational, not gimmicky.
> Working subtitle: *"The 90-Day English Drama."* (Episode 10 keeps the title **"Two Worlds"**
> as the season's thematic capstone.)

**Locked North Star:** American English is the taught target. Nour models it. Macal embodies the learner journey. Mahmoud teaches in Arabic. Dubai is the stage. Season 1 = 10 episodes. Tight 5–10 minutes, target ~7 minutes.

---

## 1. One-Line Premise + Why It Works

**Premise:** In Dubai, Egyptian expat Macal has 90 days to turn his imperfect English into a real future—keep his job, win the Rising Voices fellowship, and launch Macal Empire—while navigating a rivalry with polished Tarek, a growing connection with American-born Nour, and a family back home who believes he’s already made it.

**Why it works:**
- **Binge engine:** Every episode ends with a real choice or threat: visa, family, rivalry, love, dream.
- **Learning engine:** Every scene creates a language need. Macal must speak to survive the moment.
- **Dubai realism:** Taxi drivers, landlords, baristas, networking rooms, visa paperwork—all real.
- **Accent modeling:** Nour models warm General American. Guests keep Dubai-real accents. Macal’s accent evolves across locked stages.
- **Honesty:** No shame. Mistakes are data. The system is step by step, consistent, confidence-building.

## 2. Cast & Character Profiles

### Macal — Egyptian Learner-Hero (MACAL EMPIRE brand)
- **Age/Origin:** 27, from Alexandria, Egypt. Lives in Al Nahda, Dubai.
- **Work:** Community host/assistant at Dune & Co., a creative coworking studio. He is on probation, not a manager.
- **Backstory:** Came to Dubai two years ago to build something real. Sends half his salary home. Promised his late father he wouldn’t just survive.
- **Goal:** Win the Rising Voices fellowship, get full-time visa sponsorship, and launch Macal Empire—a small studio that fixes phones and tells expat stories.
- **Flaw:** Translates from Arabic, avoids speaking until it’s “perfect,” treats mistakes as shame.
- **Hidden motive/secret:** His family believes he is already a manager at Dune & Co. The lie is small but growing. If his mother visits, it collapses.
- **Voice evolution:**  
  - **S1 (Eps 1–3):** Egyptian-accented English, hesitant.  
  - **S2 (Eps 4–7):** More fluent, more confident.  
  - **S3 (Eps 8–10):** Confident and fluent — jokes, negotiates.
- **VOICE-PRODUCTION NOTE (locked in Phase B):** Macal is voiced by **VoiceTut ("Abdullah")**,
  which keeps a **real Egyptian accent across ALL stages**. His growth is shown through
  **language** — fluency, contractions, confidence, complexity — **not an accent shift**.
  (This is truer to real adult learning and honors the brand rule against promising a
  "native accent." The stages below describe LANGUAGE progression; the accent stays Egyptian.)
- **Function:** The learner’s heart. His English growth is earned, not magic.

### Nour — American-Born Target-Accent Model
- **Age/Origin:** 29, born and raised in Chicago, USA. General American accent. **Not British, not Egyptian-raised.**
- **Work:** Creative strategist at Dune & Co.
- **Backstory:** Moved to Dubai on a two-year contract to build a storytelling community for expats. She is good at fixing other people’s lives.
- **Goal:** Make Rising Voices a real platform. Decide whether to stay in Dubai or return to Chicago for a safe corporate role.
- **Flaw:** Avoids her own decisions by managing everyone else’s.
- **Hidden motive/secret:** She has a Chicago job offer waiting. She also secretly recommended Macal for Rising Voices—and hasn’t told him because she fears favoritism and is starting to feel something.
- **Function:** Models warm, clear, confident American English in live scenes. She is never a formal teacher.

### Mahmoud — Coach/Host
- **Role:** Egyptian-Arabic teacher and founder-style mentor. **Not a character inside the story.**
- **Backstory:** Taught English in Cairo and Dubai. Once froze in a life-changing interview because he feared mistakes. Built EEC so learners don’t face that freeze alone.
- **Goal:** Make English usable, step by step. Not perfect. Clear.
- **Flaw:** Can over-explain; checks himself with warmth and humor.
- **Hidden motive/secret (production-level):** He is quietly building this season as a bridge for learners who feel stuck between two worlds. Not revealed in-story. It fuels his patience and honesty.
- **Voice:** Arabic in coach sections. Warm, direct, encouraging. **He introduces himself by name in every coach_intro.** No shaming.

### Recurring Guests
- **Tarek El-Masry — The Rival.** 30, Egyptian, polished English, fast and impressive. Backstory: Cairo, AUC, came to Dubai to support family after father’s stroke. Goal: Win the same fellowship for security. Flaw: Needs to win; hides vulnerability behind charm. Hidden: Terrified of failing his family. May become an ally.
- **Ravi Menon — Taxi Driver.** 42, Indian from Kerala. Indian English. In Dubai 12 years. Goal: save for daughter’s nursing college. Flaw: cuts people off when traffic is bad. Hidden: writes Malayalam poetry; tests Macal’s small talk with kindness.
- **Aisha Santos — Barista.** 24, Filipina from Manila. Filipino English. Works at Common Grounds café. Goal: become café manager or move to Canada. Flaw: over-apologizes. Hidden: studying for IELTS secretly; practices English with Nour.
- **Mr. Qureshi — Landlord/Building Manager.** 55, Pakistani. Pakistani English. In Dubai since 1985. Goal: retire in Lahore. Flaw: rigid about rules. Hidden: sees his estranged son in Macal; tests him about responsibility.
- **Ms. Farida Al-Mansoori — Emirati Client/HR Lead.** 40, Gulf English, dry humor. Goal: find real talent, not polished pitches. Flaw: impatient with performance. Hidden: bored of safe answers; wants truth.

## 4. Format & Production Shape

**6-section shape. Tight 5–10 min. Target ~7 min.**

| Section | Time | Language | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **cold_open** | 0:00–0:45 | Pure English | Hook. Drop us into a live Dubai moment. | **No coach, no Arabic.** Ends on a question/conflict. |
| **coach_intro** | 0:45–1:30 | Arabic | Mahmoud introduces himself by name. Sets the language mission. | Warm, brief. |
| **act1** | 1:30–3:00 | English | Scene escalates. Target phrases appear naturally. | **Teaching never interrupts a live scene.** |
| **coach_break1** | 3:00–4:00 | Arabic | Unpack 2–3 phrases + 1 mistake. | Short, clear, no shaming. |
| **act2** | 4:00–6:00 | English | Resolution + cliffhanger. | Target phrases reused by Macal in a new way. |
| **coach_outro** | 6:00–7:00 | Arabic | Recap, Phrase of the Episode, CTA. | End with confidence. |

**Rules:**
- American English is the target. Nour models General American.
- Guests keep Dubai-real accents: Indian English, Pakistani English, Filipino English, Gulf English, Egyptian English.
- Macal’s accent evolves in locked voice stages.
- Scenes are mostly English. Coach sections are Arabic.
- Cliffhangers are story-driven, not language quizzes.
- No forbidden marketing: no “hack,” no “secret” gimmick, no “guaranteed,” no “fluent in X days,” no shaming.
- Use: system, step by step, real, consistency, confidence.

## 6. Tone, Rules & Signatures

### Tone
Balanced. Real, believable, warm, funny, honest. Genuine dramatic tension with continuing threads—but **English learning always stays the star**. Never silly, never soap-opera-cheap. Dubai is not an exotic backdrop; it is a character.

### Honesty Guardrails
- **Use:** system, step by step, real, consistency, confidence.
- **Never:** “hack,” “secret” gimmick, “guaranteed,” “fluent in X days,” shaming.
- Mistakes are normal. Confusion is normal. Confidence is built.

### Teaching Rules
- American English is the target.
- Nour models General American. Guests model Dubai-real Englishes.
- Macal’s mistakes are expected and used for teaching.
- Coach breaks unpack 2–3 phrases + 1 mistake.
- Teaching never interrupts a live scene.
- Phrases are immediately reused in act2.

### Recurring Signatures
- **Mahmoud’s Open (Arabic):**  
  “أهلاً بيكم في Yalla Fluent. أنا محمود، مدربكم في Empire English Community. النهاردة مش هنتعلم كلمات وبس—هنعيشها مع ماكال. يلا بينا.”
- **Mahmoud’s Close (Arabic):**  
  “خد الجملة دي معاك. استخدمها مرة واحدة النهاردة، مش عشان تكون perfect—عشان تكون واضح. أنا محمود، وأشوفكم المرة الجاية.”
- **Phrase of the Episode:** One high-use American English phrase. Used in scene, unpacked, then recited at the end.
- **One Mistake:** One common Arabic-speaker mistake, corrected gently.
- **Real-Life Challenge CTA:** “Use it once today. Not to be perfect. To be clear.”

### Drama + Teaching Balance
- Drama asks: *What does Macal need to say now?*
- Teaching answers: *Here is how to say it.*
- Cliffhangers create the next language need.
- No vocabulary detours. No grammar lectures longer than 60 seconds.
- Emotion is in English. Explanation is in Arabic.

## 8. Scripting Craft (how every episode is written)

> These rules make each ~7-minute episode tight, teachable, and dramatic. They mirror
> the spec (`design.md` §3.4/§3.5) and govern both generation and review.

### Timing & word budget (in-media-res)
- ~7 min ≈ **~400 words of spoken English** total across the story scenes.
- Per-section budget: cold_open ≈ 45–70w · act1 ≈ 150–190w · act2 ≈ 120–170w
  (Arabic coach sections are separate, paced ~150 wpm).
- **Start in-media-res** — cold_open and act1 drop into a scene already in motion. No
  throat-clearing, no "hello, my name is." Exposition comes through conflict.

### Macal's voice-stage script markers (written into the text)
- **S1 (eps 1–3):** uncontracted forms ("I am not sure"), explicit micro-pauses `...`,
  simple tenses, deliberate earnest rhythm.
- **S2 (eps 4–7):** contractions ("I've been"), self-corrections ("I mean…"), basic
  linking, emerging present-perfect/conditionals.
- **S3 (eps 8–10):** natural reductions in casual lines ("gonna", "wanna"), native-like
  sentence stress, can joke and negotiate.
- **Pedagogy caveat:** Macal's scripted English is **grammatical-but-accented** (pauses,
  uncontracted forms, rhythm). Learners imitate what they hear, so we do NOT scatter
  broken English — **real errors are reserved for the one planted mistake** (below).

### The mistake → correction → triumph loop (REQUIRED every episode)
1. **act1:** Macal makes ONE realistic L2 mistake for his stage (often an Arabic-structure
   transfer, e.g. "I live here since two years").
2. **coach_break1:** Mahmoud unpacks 2–3 target phrases AND corrects that mistake,
   explaining **why** it happens (the Arabic→English transfer) before the natural
   American form. Under 60 seconds, zero shaming.
3. **act2:** Macal **reuses the corrected form** in a new context as a **triumph beat** —
   the correction becomes a character win, not a grammar note.

### Target phrases & guest accents
- 2–3 high-use American phrases per episode: land naturally in act1, unpacked in
  coach_break1, reused in act2, recited as the "Phrase of the Episode" in coach_outro.
- Guests keep authentic Dubai-real accents (Indian/Filipino/Pakistani/Gulf) but stay
  **clear and A2/B1-accessible** — no dense slang, no rapid-fire. Focus stays on Macal + Nour.

### Per-section QA checklist
| Section | Drama objective | Language objective | Quality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold_open | Immediate friction, in-media-res | Expose the target situation | Zero coach/Arabic? Ends on a question or conflict? |
| act1 | Escalate the conflict | Deliver 2–3 target phrases naturally | One realistic L2 mistake tied to Macal's stage? |
| coach_break1 | Teaching beat | Unpack phrases + correct the mistake, explain WHY | ≤60s, zero shaming, correction ties to act1? |
| act2 | Resolve + cliffhanger | Reuse the phrase in a new context | Macal applies the correction as a triumph beat? |
| coach_outro | Wrap | Recap + Phrase of the Episode + CTA | Ends on confidence; cliffhanger story-driven, not a quiz? |

## 10. STRUCTURE RULE — story vs. teaching separation (LOCKED, machine-enforced)

> Why this exists: an episode is audio. If the Coach's Arabic commentary is
> sprinkled *inside* a live English scene, the listener gets language whiplash
> (English tension → sudden Arabic aside → English) and it sounds messy. The fix
> is a hard rule, enforced by `pipeline/structure_check.py` at generation time AND
> before assembly — a violating script can never be assembled/shipped.

**Two kinds of section:**
- **STORY sections** (in-world scene, English drama): `cold_open`, `act1`, `act2`,
  `act3`, `act4`.
- **COACH sections** (teaching, Egyptian Arabic): `coach_intro`, `coach_break1`,
  `coach_break2`, `coach_break3`, `coach_outro`.

**The rules (all enforced):**
1. **`cold_open` is a PURE story hook** — only in-world characters (Macal, guests),
   English. **No Coach line, no Arabic, in the cold_open.** The Coach first speaks
   in `coach_intro`.
2. **The Coach speaks ONLY in COACH sections.** No Coach line may appear inside any
   STORY section. Teaching happens in its own beat, never mid-scene.
3. **Story characters speak ONLY in STORY sections.** No in-world dialogue leaks into
   a COACH section.
4. **Canonical section order** (a scene, then its breakdown, repeating):
   `cold_open → coach_intro → act1 → coach_break1 → act2 → coach_break2 → act3 →
   coach_break3 → act4 → coach_outro`. (Episodes may use fewer acts/breaks, but a
   COACH break must follow the STORY scene it explains — never split a scene.)
5. A COACH breakdown may be several consecutive Coach lines (that's fine — it's one
   teaching beat); what's forbidden is putting those lines *inside* a story section's
   idx range.

**Result:** playback is always "full English scene → clean cut to Coach breakdown →
next scene," which is the professional format the series was designed around (§5).
